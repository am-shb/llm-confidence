# Deviations from PROTOCOL.md

Anything that changes after the protocol commit goes here: date, what changed, why.
Empty is the expected state.

| Date | Section | Change | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | Qwen arms pinned to `Parasail` (fp8) instead of the declared first-party `Alibaba` endpoint. | `Alibaba` advertises `logprobs` but not `structured_outputs`, so it cannot serve Q-V under section 4's structured-outputs rule; it could serve only Q-L. No endpoint of the 16 offers unquantized + logprobs + structured outputs together (`DeepInfra` is the sole bf16 endpoint and has no logprobs). `Parasail` supports both mechanisms at 100% uptime, keeping Q-V and Q-L on one identical backend, which is the property C3 depends on. Consequence: section 9's quantization limitation sharpens — C3 is an fp8 estimate and the post must say so. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | F1-V pinned to first-party `Anthropic`; F2-V pinned to first-party `OpenAI`. | Section 4 requires structured outputs on every verbalized arm, but only 2 of 10 `claude-sonnet-5` endpoints support them (`Anthropic`, `Claude Platform on AWS`) and the Bedrock `gpt-5.6-terra` endpoint does not. Without an explicit pin, routing could silently drop constrained decoding and move parse failures into the primary metric. Section 3's claim that no frontier provider exposes answer-token logprobs was re-verified at the same time and holds: 0 of 10 Sonnet and 0 of 7 Terra endpoints list `logprobs`. |
| 2026-09-19 | 3, 4 | Arm J is called via `POST /api/alpha/decisions` with a typed-decision schema (`state` + `questions`/`criteria`), not the chat/completions endpoint the other five arms use. | J speaks TypeSafe's documented "System One" decisions contract (https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/quickstart), not chat/completions: typesafe/jev-1.13 rejects chat/completions outright with HTTP 400 ("is a decisions model and cannot be used with the chat/completions endpoint"), and all four candidate chat shapes (plain, tool-required, tool-named, json-schema) failed identically. We reach that contract through OpenRouter's /api/alpha/decisions passthrough rather than TypeSafe's first-party endpoint, because section 3 pins the exact version typesafe/jev-1.13 while the first-party API documents jev-latest as its default; the exact pin is load-bearing for section 2's contamination argument. Section 4's structured-outputs and provider-pin language therefore does not apply to J in the form written: its schema has no response_format and no provider/allow_fallbacks field, and TypeSafe is its only provider (the response echoes provider: "TypeSafe", which is checked instead). Section 4 content parity is preserved — J receives the same question text, the same verbatim category descriptions as criteria, and the same per-item option permutation via criteria insertion order — but the framing differs structurally and the post states that rather than glossing it. |
| 2026-09-19 | 3 | J's endpoint is labelled alpha (`/api/alpha/decisions`). | Recorded as a reproducibility risk: an alpha contract may change without notice, so a later re-run could fail or behave differently even with the model pin unchanged. The raw responses saved under `probes/` and `runs/` are the durable record of what the contract was when the study ran. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | The Qwen arms now send `reasoning: {"enabled": false}` explicitly. | Section 4 requires the Qwen arms to stay non-thinking, but non-thinking is NOT the pinned endpoint's default: the first dev run produced a `reasoning` field on 200/200 Q-L records, median 484 completion tokens for a one-letter answer, and a 14x cost overrun. The logprobs being read were therefore those of the first token AFTER a chain of thought rather than the single-pass readout section 4 specifies, which showed as near-deterministic output (mean top probability 0.985) and a worse Brier than the corrected run. Only `{"enabled": false}` suppresses it; `effort: "minimal"` and `max_tokens: 0` both still consume 690-805 reasoning tokens. With thinking the model answered C and without it G, so this changed answers, not merely cost. The violating data was discarded and both Qwen arms re-run. This is enforcement of section 4, not a departure from it, and is recorded because "non-thinking" could not be assumed. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4, 9 | Section 4's claim that omitting the reasoning parameter means "adaptive thinking on at effort `high`" on `claude-sonnet-5` is factually incorrect for the pinned endpoint. F1-V ran with ZERO reasoning tokens on all 200 dev items. | The procedure section 4 mandates was followed exactly — the parameter was omitted so the model runs as it ships, and the defaults in force were recorded at run time, which is what caught this. The parameter was deliberately NOT added afterwards: forcing a mistaken description to come true would be a real deviation from the binding instruction. Consequences: C1's framing "as they ship, reasoning included" is false for F1-V; and section 9's limitation "Single-pass against reasoning. F1-V and F2-V think; J cannot... it favours the baselines" is wrong for F1-V and overstated for F2-V, which used only 68 reasoning tokens per item. C1 is therefore MORE like-for-like than the protocol anticipated. Stated as a correction rather than quietly benefited from. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | Letter-position bias in Q-L is present and is reported, not corrected. | Section 4 says the per-item option shuffle "also controls letter-position bias in Q-L". Measured on dev, the chosen letters A-G were 18/26/13/49/24/13/57, chi-square 64.7 against uniform, p about 5e-12. Our own shuffle was verified uniform independently (label x letter-position over 49 cells, p about 0.85), so the bias is the model's, not the sampler's. The accurate reading of section 4 is that the shuffle prevents the bias from systematically favouring any one LABEL, which is what the metrics require; it does not eliminate the bias, which remains a noise source specific to Q-L. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | `max_tokens` is not set on any arm. | Section 4 states as a requirement: "`max_tokens` is capped, but for F1-V and F2-V the cap must clear observed dev-run thinking plus answer length with margin." No arm sets it. Zero truncations occurred across 1,000 dev calls, and a cap chosen from dev observations could only penalise the arms it was meant to protect if evaluation-set responses ran longer. Truncation remains a section 5 failure if it ever occurs, and is counted per arm. Recorded as a deviation from the pre-registration rather than, as `FREEZE.md` first framed it, a departure from the implementation plan. |
| 2026-09-19 | 5 | One F1-V dev record was DELETED from the append-only log and the item re-run, rather than being scored as uniform. | Section 5 says failures "are scored as uniform and counted per arm. Never dropped." The record was an HTTP 402: the account was within $1.77 of its credit limit mid-run. Section 5's enumerated failure kinds are all model behaviours — parse error, refusal, truncation, non-finite or negative value, a letter outside A-G, timeout after 3 retries — and a billing exhaustion is none of them; scoring it uniform would have charged the arm for our account state. This is a substantive reinterpretation of the binding spec and is recorded as such. The run-level failure path has since been changed in code so a funding failure ABORTS the run instead of being written as data, and so a resume retries non-model failures. |
| 2026-09-19 | 2, 3 | Arm J's pin `typesafe/jev-1.13` resolves to the dated build `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`, echoed by all 200 dev responses. | Section 3 asserts "the pin is exact." It is exact as a request, but it resolves to a snapshot stamped 2026-09-17 — INSIDE the harvest window of 20 Aug to 17 Sept and two days before the harvest. Section 2 calls J's pre-September training cutoff "the weakest load-bearing assumption in the protocol" and pre-commits that results for J are void if the cutoff falls inside the evaluation window. This build string is the most direct evidence the repository holds on that question, so it is recorded here rather than left in the raw logs. It does not by itself establish the cutoff: a build date is when weights were packaged, not when training data ends. TypeSafe has still not been asked. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | Batch endpoints were not used. | Section 4 says "Batch endpoints where available." Availability was not established for the pinned endpoints, and the dev pass completed comfortably without them. Recorded so the choice is visible rather than assumed. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | Q-L's effective sampling temperature is the provider's default and was not recorded. | Section 4 correctly forbids setting temperature on Q-L, and says "The defaults in force are recorded at run time." That discipline was applied to F1-V and F2-V's reasoning defaults but not to Q-L's sampling default. Evidence that the default is non-zero: the sampled first token differs from the argmax of the returned top-k on 60 of 200 dev items. If the backend applies logits processors before returning logprobs, Q-L's read distribution may be sharpened or flattened by an unknown constant, which would confound C3 and C4. Unresolved; settleable for a fraction of a cent by comparing an explicit `temperature: 1.0` against an omitted temperature on one dev item at the same seed. |
| 2026-09-19 | 5 | `MAX_ATTEMPTS` is 3 attempts, i.e. 2 retries, where section 5 says "timeout after 3 retries." | Literal divergence with no effect on any result: the item is recorded as a failure and scored uniform either way. Recorded for completeness. |
| 2026-09-20 | — | Git history was rewritten once, before this repository's first push, to remove an OpenRouter account identifier from two intermediate commits. | The id appeared in `probes/jev_probe.json` in commits `f47c9cc` and `5b6a970` (pre-rewrite hashes), before a later commit redacted it. Section 4 commits to publishing the raw responses and the repository is public, so the identifier would have become permanently public via history even though the final content was already clean. It is an identifier, not a credential: it cannot authenticate or spend. The rewrite altered only historical blobs — every tree's final state is byte-identical, verified by diffing the rewritten tip against a pre-rewrite backup. Cost: all commit hashes from the probe commit onward changed, so hashes cited in earlier commit messages no longer resolve; `FREEZE.md` section 7 carries the mapping for the ones referenced in the record. `PROTOCOL.md` was untouched and remains byte-identical to its pre-registration commit, which is the property the study's checkability rests on — the pre-registration's content, not its hash. |
| 2026-09-20 | 7 | Section 7's "top-label ECE" and section 6's acceptance threshold tau are computed from `max(probabilities)` for every arm. Arm J alone also exposes a distinct scalar `confidence` field, so for J both bases are reported. | The pre-registration predates knowledge of the decisions contract and says only "top-label ECE", which is unambiguous for the four chat arms but not for J: J returns `probabilities` (a 7-vector), `choice`, AND a separate `confidence` scalar that is NOT the maximum of the vector — the two disagree on 1,338 of 2,000 evaluation items, with `confidence` systematically lower (mean 0.840 against 0.865). Using `max(probabilities)` keeps every arm on one definition, which is what a cross-arm comparison requires; but if any calibration claim attaches to Jev it most plausibly attaches to the field literally named `confidence`, so reporting only the former would invite the objection that the wrong field was measured. Both are therefore reported for J, and the conclusion does not depend on the choice: strict ECE is 0.1337 from `max(probabilities)` and 0.1089 from `confidence`, both above section 8's 0.05 threshold. The multiclass Brier, the calibration gap and every contrast are unaffected, since a scalar cannot produce the K-vector a proper scoring rule requires. |
| 2026-09-20 | 7 | Latency p50/p95 is reported from the main concurrent runs, not from a dedicated 200-item sequential subsample. | Section 7 asks for "latency p50/p95 from Toronto on a 200-item sequential subsample". The arms were run concurrently (4-6 workers) and, for F1-V, additionally throttled to a 3.2-second minimum inter-request interval to stay under a provider grammar-compilation cap. Per-item `latency_s` is recorded for every call but reflects queueing under concurrency and, for F1-V, deliberate throttling, so it is NOT comparable across arms and is NOT a clean measure of single-request latency. Reported with that caveat rather than presented as the section 7 figure. A dedicated sequential subsample was not run; the remaining credit was reserved for completing the evaluation arms. |
| 2026-09-20 | 7 | The epsilon-sensitivity recomputation re-parses from the raw JSONL rather than reading the stored prediction arrays. | `preds/*.npz` holds POST-floor vectors, so re-flooring them at a different epsilon does not reproduce what direct flooring of the raw response would give: at epsilon 1e-4 it is an exact no-op (the stored minimum already exceeds 1e-4) and would silently return a figure identical to the baseline, looking like a robustness result; at 1e-2 it picks up a spurious factor. The analysis therefore reads `runs/*.jsonl` and applies each epsilon to the raw probabilities once. Recorded because the wrong implementation fails silently rather than loudly. |
