# Deviations from PROTOCOL.md

Anything that changes after the protocol commit goes here: date, what changed, why.
Empty is the expected state.

| Date | Section | Change | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | Qwen arms pinned to `Parasail` (fp8) instead of the declared first-party `Alibaba` endpoint. | `Alibaba` advertises `logprobs` but not `structured_outputs`, so it cannot serve Q-V under section 4's structured-outputs rule; it could serve only Q-L. No endpoint of the 16 offers unquantized + logprobs + structured outputs together (`DeepInfra` is the sole bf16 endpoint and has no logprobs). `Parasail` supports both mechanisms at 100% uptime, keeping Q-V and Q-L on one identical backend, which is the property C3 depends on. Consequence: section 9's quantization limitation sharpens — C3 is an fp8 estimate and the post must say so. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | F1-V pinned to first-party `Anthropic`; F2-V pinned to first-party `OpenAI`. | Section 4 requires structured outputs on every verbalized arm, but only 2 of 10 `claude-sonnet-5` endpoints support them (`Anthropic`, `Claude Platform on AWS`) and the Bedrock `gpt-5.6-terra` endpoint does not. Without an explicit pin, routing could silently drop constrained decoding and move parse failures into the primary metric. Section 3's claim that no frontier provider exposes answer-token logprobs was re-verified at the same time and holds: 0 of 10 Sonnet and 0 of 7 Terra endpoints list `logprobs`. |
| 2026-09-19 | 3, 4 | Arm J is called via `POST /api/alpha/decisions` with a typed-decision schema (`state` + `questions`/`criteria`), not the chat/completions endpoint the other five arms use. | J speaks TypeSafe's documented "System One" decisions contract (https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/quickstart), not chat/completions: typesafe/jev-1.13 rejects chat/completions outright with HTTP 400 ("is a decisions model and cannot be used with the chat/completions endpoint"), and all four candidate chat shapes (plain, tool-required, tool-named, json-schema) failed identically. We reach that contract through OpenRouter's /api/alpha/decisions passthrough rather than TypeSafe's first-party endpoint, because section 3 pins the exact version typesafe/jev-1.13 while the first-party API documents jev-latest as its default; the exact pin is load-bearing for section 2's contamination argument. Section 4's structured-outputs and provider-pin language therefore does not apply to J in the form written: its schema has no response_format and no provider/allow_fallbacks field, and TypeSafe is its only provider (the response echoes provider: "TypeSafe", which is checked instead). Section 4 content parity is preserved — J receives the same question text, the same verbatim category descriptions as criteria, and the same per-item option permutation via criteria insertion order — but the framing differs structurally and the post states that rather than glossing it. |
| 2026-09-19 | 3 | J's endpoint is labelled alpha (`/api/alpha/decisions`). | Recorded as a reproducibility risk: an alpha contract may change without notice, so a later re-run could fail or behave differently even with the model pin unchanged. The raw responses saved under `probes/` and `runs/` are the durable record of what the contract was when the study ran. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | The Qwen arms now send `reasoning: {"enabled": false}` explicitly. | Section 4 requires the Qwen arms to stay non-thinking, but non-thinking is NOT the pinned endpoint's default: the first dev run produced a `reasoning` field on 200/200 Q-L records, median 484 completion tokens for a one-letter answer, and a 14x cost overrun. The logprobs being read were therefore those of the first token AFTER a chain of thought rather than the single-pass readout section 4 specifies, which showed as near-deterministic output (mean top probability 0.985) and a worse Brier than the corrected run. Only `{"enabled": false}` suppresses it; `effort: "minimal"` and `max_tokens: 0` both still consume 690-805 reasoning tokens. With thinking the model answered C and without it G, so this changed answers, not merely cost. The violating data was discarded and both Qwen arms re-run. This is enforcement of section 4, not a departure from it, and is recorded because "non-thinking" could not be assumed. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4, 9 | Section 4's claim that omitting the reasoning parameter means "adaptive thinking on at effort `high`" on `claude-sonnet-5` is factually incorrect for the pinned endpoint. F1-V ran with ZERO reasoning tokens on all 200 dev items. | The procedure section 4 mandates was followed exactly — the parameter was omitted so the model runs as it ships, and the defaults in force were recorded at run time, which is what caught this. The parameter was deliberately NOT added afterwards: forcing a mistaken description to come true would be a real deviation from the binding instruction. Consequences: C1's framing "as they ship, reasoning included" is false for F1-V; and section 9's limitation "Single-pass against reasoning. F1-V and F2-V think; J cannot... it favours the baselines" is wrong for F1-V and overstated for F2-V, which used only 68 reasoning tokens per item. C1 is therefore MORE like-for-like than the protocol anticipated. Stated as a correction rather than quietly benefited from. |
| 2026-09-19 | 4 | Letter-position bias in Q-L is present and is reported, not corrected. | Section 4 says the per-item option shuffle "also controls letter-position bias in Q-L". Measured on dev, the chosen letters A-G were 18/26/13/49/24/13/57, chi-square 64.7 against uniform, p about 5e-12. Our own shuffle was verified uniform independently (label x letter-position over 49 cells, p about 0.85), so the bias is the model's, not the sampler's. The accurate reading of section 4 is that the shuffle prevents the bias from systematically favouring any one LABEL, which is what the metrics require; it does not eliminate the bias, which remains a noise source specific to Q-L. |
